Figure 5 | Six improvement regimes of material provisioning, 2000-2020.

Each panel is one regime; each dot is one of eleven decent-living indicators. Improvement
rates are ordinary-least-squares slopes on year over 2000-2020, in index points per year,
averaged over the fifteen multiply-imputed datasets. Because a country already close to
the ceiling on a service cannot improve on it as fast as one starting from a low base,
each rate is residualised on a cubic polynomial in that country's own year-2000 value of
the same indicator; the plotted quantity is the regime mean of that residual. Zero
therefore means a regime advanced that component exactly as fast as countries starting
from the same position typically did, filled dots to the right mark faster-than-expected
progress, and open dots to the left mark slower-than-expected progress. This is the space
in which the regimes were formed: they are the groups obtained by Ward minimum-variance
clustering on Euclidean distances between these standardised residuals, with the number
of groups chosen by the C-index and the ensemble partition taken as the Mirkin medoid of
the fifteen imputation-specific partitions. Table 4 reports the same improvement rates
unadjusted, in raw points per year; the raw and adjusted quantities disagree in sign for
eleven of the sixty-six regime-indicator cells, so the table and this figure are not
alternative displays of one quantity and should be read together. The eleven indicators
appear in the same order in every panel, grouped by decent-living-standards domain, and
all six panels share one horizontal scale. Regimes are ordered by mean development level,
the regime mean of the country's average standardised year-2000 value across the eleven
indicators, shown in each header with the regime's size and four example countries drawn
from the 12th, 38th, 62nd and 88th percentile of that regime's own level distribution.
N = 173 countries.
